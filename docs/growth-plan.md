# Western growth line: decision log + pathway to the first $10,000

Last updated: 2026-07-16. This is the working memory for the second product line (paid live online yoga + AI-assisted form feedback, sold to US/Europe customers who can't get to a studio). See `CLAUDE.md` for where this sits relative to the core India-market site.

## 1. First principles (read this before re-litigating any decision below)

- **Komal is the scarce resource.** One person, one face, one Instagram identity, fixed teaching hours. The plan picks one ICP at a time, not a portfolio, because a solo brand can't credibly hold multiple specialist identities at once.
- **Trust is the product.** Nobody pays a recurring fee for something free alternatives already offer, unless they believe a real, qualified human is paying attention to them specifically. This is why the AI is marketed honestly (async, human-reviewed) in every version of this plan, cautious or aggressive — overclaiming it doesn't just risk regulatory trouble, it removes the actual reason to pay.
- **Demand is measured, not inferred from need.** Severity of need (how much someone needs yoga) and size of findable, monetizable demand (how many people search for it) are different variables. Ranking ICPs by empathy produced the wrong answer; real search-volume data corrected it.
- **A free incumbent changes what you're allowed to sell.** Any large ICP with a subsidized free option (SilverSneakers, YMCA) can only be beaten on something free can't offer: personal attention, a named teacher, a gift you give someone else.
- **Payment rails are upstream of marketing.** An India-based business selling to Western customers can't assume its default payment setup reaches those customers. This has to be resolved before funnel/marketing work, not after.
- **Claims are bounded by capability and credentials, and this boundary doesn't move by picking a different ICP.** Bee's real state (async, human-reviewed, no live multi-user mode) caps what can be said about "AI feedback," period. Komal's actual certifications cap what conditions she can credibly claim to serve. Every ICP considered so far (postpartum, seniors, adaptive) has hit this same second ceiling — see section 2.

### Postnatal self-study roadmap (background track, verified resources only)

1. **Weeks 1-4, free**: [ACOG's postpartum exercise guidance](https://www.acog.org/womens-health/faqs/exercise-after-pregnancy); free professional webinars from [Core Exercise Solutions](https://www.coreexercisesolutions.com/free-courses/) (run by a DPT); the *Yoga | Birth | Babies* podcast.
2. **Month 2**: Leslie Howard's free pelvic-floor yoga guide (via YogaUOnline).
3. **Months 2-5, ~$499**: [Island Prenatal Yoga's 50-hour self-paced online Prenatal & Postnatal YTT](https://www.islandprenatalyoga.com/prenatal-postnatal-ytt) — Yoga Alliance-recognized, covers postnatal teaching specifically.
4. **Months 5-8+, optional, ~$799**: their 85-hour RPYT track for the fuller credential.

Realistic timeline: 3-4 months to responsibly offer postnatal-informed modifications *while disclosing it's in progress*; 6-9 months for a full credential. Keep disclosing in-progress status until the certificate is actually in hand.

### Correction: the "no one combines live + personal" whitespace claim was too strong

A follow-up US market pass (offline + online pricing, real numbers) found **free, live, real-instructor chair yoga for seniors already exists** — GetSetUp, Bold, Adaptive Yoga LIVE, AARP Senior Planet, alongside SilverSneakers/Renew Active (free via many Medicare Advantage plans). This directly undercuts the earlier competitive-landscape finding that nobody combines a live human teacher with this audience — several nonprofit/insurance-subsidized platforms already do, for free, permanently (subsidized, not a launch-phase discount).

Applying the Delta 4 framework (people only act when the gap between current and promised state feels large enough, weighted by certainty/speed) honestly: **on the "live + personal" axis, the delta against these specific free competitors is close to zero.** What survives is narrower — the $49 course is a purchasable, ownable, gift-able product none of the free platforms offer (they're ongoing class access, not something you buy once and hand to someone), and the AI-assisted form review is a real feature none of them have. The honest positioning is "something you can buy today and hand to your parent, with a form-check add-on," not "the only live option" — and marketing copy should say so plainly rather than let a prospective customer discover the free alternative themselves and lose trust in the process. The `/chair-yoga-for-seniors` page has been updated to name these competitors directly rather than only the weaker (recorded/generic-access) comparisons.

## 3. Product & pricing (revenue-max structure)

The lead offer is **not** the subscription. Cold Instagram traffic buys a one-time gift for someone else ("for my mom"); it doesn't buy a stranger's recurring subscription. Structure:

- **$49 "Chair Yoga Foundations"** — self-paced digital course, the lead/tripwire offer.
- **+$19 order bump** — seated-routines/props pack, ~25-30% attach assumed.
- **$89/mo membership** ($69/mo introductory rate while the class is still filling toward capacity, locked in once you join) — positioned as the *upsell* after course purchase, not the storefront lead. Deliberately **not** framed as a "founding cohort" or a manufactured headcount ("first 10!") — the content-strategy research for this audience explicitly found trust-first, no-urgency/no-hype marketing converts better here, since the entire reason to pay is belief in a real attentive teacher, not FOMO. The discount is tied to something true (the class is genuinely capacity-limited, see below) instead of a manufactured number.
- **$59 1:1 session** — supplementary, high-margin, hard-capped at 1 slot/day (see operating constraint below), not a volume line.
- **Explicit "gift this to your parent" purchase flow** — the actual wedge against free incumbents (SilverSneakers can't be gifted meaningfully).

AI framing stays exactly as honest as in the cautious plan: "Personal feedback on every session you submit, AI-assisted, verified by Komal, within 24 hours." Never "real-time," never implying monitoring/fall-detection, never a health-outcome claim (arthritis relief, fall prevention) — those trigger FTC scrutiny that specifically watches marketing to seniors.

### Operating capacity constraint (hard rule, not a suggestion)

Komal teaches **at most 2 live sessions a day: one group class, one 1:1.** This isn't a soft target, it's the actual answer to "why does the plan include 1:1 sessions at all — isn't her time limited?" Yes. So the plan has to be built around that limit explicitly, not around a revenue target that assumes unlimited hours.

Assumption used below (adjustable — confirm with Komal): **5 teaching days/week (Mon-Fri)**, weekends unscheduled for rest, content, and credentialing study.

**Two different numbers, don't conflate them:**
- **Per-session cap (~10 people)** — a real, teaching-quality constraint. Komal has to actually see and give feedback to everyone live; this is the whole differentiator against a free, impersonal video. Enforced per-day via Cal.com booking.
- **Total membership size (~15-18 people)** — not the same number. The class runs 5x/week and members typically attend 2-3x/week, not daily, so total membership can exceed the per-session cap without any single session overflowing, as long as a booking tool hard-caps each day. (An earlier pass at this doc wrongly set the total membership cap equal to the per-session cap — that's fixed here.)

| Line | Capacity | Monthly ceiling |
|---|---|---|
| Group class | 1 session/day × 5 days/week ≈ 21-22 sessions/month, ~10 people/session hard cap, ~15-18 total members before overflow risk | ~16 members blended $69-89 ≈ **~$1,200/mo** |
| 1:1 | 1 slot/day × 5 days/week ≈ 21-22 slots/month | Fully booked: 22 × $59 ≈ **~$1,280/mo** |
| **Live teaching total, fully booked** | | **~$2,480/mo** |

That's the ceiling. No matter how much demand exists, live teaching cannot generate much more than roughly **$2,500/month** under this rule, full stop. This is exactly why the course has to be the volume driver, not a nice-to-have: it's the only line in the model that isn't capped by Komal's hours. Section 5 is rebuilt around this.

## 4. Payments (open, blocking)

A Stripe account exists. Unresolved: its registration country and whether it's already processed international (non-INR) charges.

- **If non-India-registered and already live for international cards**: use Stripe directly. Checkout + Billing for one-off + recurring, Stripe Tax on for VAT/sales tax, Customer Portal for self-serve cancellation. Simpler than the alternative below.
- **If India-registered**: still hits the constraint the original research found — invite-only accounts, ~6% all-in cross-border fees (4.3% cross-border + 2% FX + 18% GST on Stripe's fees), no native EU local payment methods (iDEAL, SEPA). Paddle as merchant-of-record remains the fallback (~5% + 50¢/transaction, handles global cards/currency/tax remittance without needing India Stripe access).

**This must be resolved before the checkout/funnel gets built** — it decides the entire payment architecture, not just a config value.

## 5. Pathway to the first $10,000

This is a model, not a guarantee. It assumes some existing organic engagement on @releaseandrealize; the actual timeline depends on her real follower count and average Reel reach, which haven't been measured (Instagram scraping was blocked by the browser extension's site permissions — a real number here would sharpen every estimate below).

**Rebuilt around the capacity ceiling in section 3, not around backing into a number.** Live teaching (group + 1:1) tops out at ~$2,480/mo *fully booked*, and won't be fully booked from week one — realistically it ramps over the first 10-12 weeks. So live teaching's honest cumulative contribution by the time the plan crosses $10k is closer to **$3,500-4,000** (membership filling toward its ~16-seat cap gradually, 1:1 slots filling gradually), not the full monthly ceiling multiplied out. The course has to carry the rest.

The course is still the volume driver, now explicitly *because* live teaching is hard-capped rather than just because it converts well. If live teaching ramps slower than assumed, the course has to cover more of the gap — course sales are the lever to pull, not more live sessions, since more live sessions isn't an option under the 2-a-day rule.

### Phased path

**Phase 0 — Infrastructure (before any revenue)**
- Resolve the Stripe/Paddle decision (section 4)
- Record the Chair Yoga Foundations course
- Build the seniors flagship page + course checkout
- Set up the DM-keyword automation (e.g. "comment CHAIR" → free 7-day starter, email-gated)

**Phase 1 — Proof, organic only (target: first ~$1,000-1,500)**
- Push existing IG audience through the DM funnel and the free starter
- Launch the gift SKU messaging explicitly ("give this to your mom")
- First course sales + first 1:1 sessions land here; this is the funnel-validation stage, not the growth stage

**Phase 2 — Recurring layer (target: cumulative ~$3,500-4,500)**
- Lock in the 10-spot founding membership cohort ($690/mo recurring installed)
- Turn on the referral loop (one free month per successful referral) — senior social graphs (church groups, senior centers, family WhatsApp chats) carry unusually high word-of-mouth density
- Keep course sales flowing via the gift SKU and referrals

**Phase 3 — Scale (target: crossing $10,000 cumulative)**
- Identify whichever Reel already organically outperforms the rest
- Put a small budget ($5-10/day) behind *that specific Reel only*, targeting women 45-60 in the US (the buyer, not the end user)
- Never pay to test unproven content — organic is the ad-creative R&D lab

The postpartum SEO cluster page (`/postpartum-yoga-diastasis-recti`) stays live as supportive content but no longer links to a paid flagship offer.

## 6. What's still genuinely unresolved

Every high-value ICP found so far (postpartum, seniors, adaptive) sits on the same fault line: real market demand exists ahead of Komal's credentialed expertise to serve it responsibly. Re-picking the ICP doesn't remove this, it only relocates it. The honest options, undisguised:

1. Build the credential first (the roadmap in section 2), accept a slower launch.
2. Launch narrower, on what she's already qualified for today — general online Hatha-Vinyasa/gentle yoga, no condition-specific claim, sacrificing some of the search-volume advantage that specific framing (postnatal, seniors) provides.
3. Launch on the claimed segment now, with the credentialing work running in parallel and disclosed as in-progress, not pretended-complete.

This decision is Komal's to make, not a research or market-sizing question.
